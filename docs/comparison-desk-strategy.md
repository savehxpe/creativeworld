# Comparison Desk Strategy

Outworld Creative — Signal Desk  
Branch: creative-director

---

## Purpose

Comparison Desk creates daily comparison-led posts that teach brands how attention, ads, sound, platforms, creators, and culture actually work.

**The format:** Compare what people think is happening vs. what is actually happening.

**The effect:** Creates the "wait, what?" energy that makes people stop, think, and share.

---

## Rules

| Do This | Don't Do This |
|---------|---------------|
| Use public facts | Use conspiracy language |
| Business logic | Claim secret control or occult influence |
| Sharp, factual, calm | Sound crazy or reckless |
| Premium, useful | Motivational fluff |
| Show the gap | Show off money or followers |

---

## The 10 Comparison Formats

### 1. Small Brand vs. Big Brand

```
Small brands post products.
Big brands build memory.
```

**The lesson:** One is trying to sell today. The other is teaching your brain what to remember tomorrow.

---

### 2. Random Content vs. Campaign System

```
Random content fills the page.
Campaign assets move the brand.
```

**The lesson:** A content creator makes the post. A creative director decides why the post should exist.

---

### 3. Random Sound vs. Brand Sound

```
Random sounds chase trends.
Brand sounds build recognition.
```

**The lesson:** A trending sound gets views today and is forgotten tomorrow. A brand sound becomes your asset.

---

### 4. Views vs. Conversion

```
Views tell you people saw it.
Bookings tell you people cared.
```

**The lesson:** 10K views with 12 bookings is worse than 1K views with 50 bookings. The gap is creative direction.

---

### 5. Content Creator vs. Creative Director

```
A content creator makes the post.
A creative director decides why the post should exist.
```

**The lesson:** Creation is a skill. Direction is a system. Brands need both, but most only have one.

---

### 6. Traditional Ad vs. UGC Ad

```
Traditional ads look approved.
UGC ads look believed.
```

**The lesson:** The best UGC doesn't look like an ad. It looks like a friend made it. That's the whole point.

---

### 7. Platform Users vs. Platform Owners

```
Users make culture.
Platforms monetize behavior.
Brands pay to enter the conversation.
```

**The lesson:** You're not just competing with other brands. You're competing with the system that decides what gets seen.

---

### 8. Attention vs. Memory

```
Attention makes people stop.
Memory makes them come back.
```

**The lesson:** A 5-second hook gets attention. A brand sound gets memory. You need both.

---

### 9. Cheap Content vs. Campaign Asset

```
Cheap content fills the page.
Campaign assets move the brand.
```

**The lesson:** Posting daily with no direction is worse than posting weekly with a system.

---

### 10. Product Post vs. Brand World

```
A product post says "buy this."
A brand world says "this is who you become."
```

**The lesson:** The best brands don't sell products. They sell identity. Then the product sells itself.

---

## How to Source Facts

**For platform ownership comparisons:**
- Use SEC filings for shareholder data
- Use public proxy statements for executive control
- Use platform public statements for algorithm explanations
- Never claim secret control or unnamed groups

**Example (clean):**
```
People think social media is controlled by creators.

Reality:
Creators create the culture.
Platforms control the rules.
Advertisers fund the attention.
Shareholders own part of the upside.
```

**Not:**
```
BlackRock runs social media.
Secret groups control what you see.
```

---

## Integration with Signal Desk

Comparison Desk is the 9th pillar of Signal Desk. It appears 1-2 times per week, not daily.

**When to post Comparison Desk:**
- When a clear industry gap is visible
- When platform news breaks (use facts, not theories)
- When brand behavior is obviously misaligned with results
- When you want to teach, not just inform
