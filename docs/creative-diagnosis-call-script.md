# Creative Diagnosis Call Script

Outworld Creative  
AI-powered video advertising studio  
Branch: creative-director

---

## Philosophy

This is not a sales call.  
This is a **creative diagnosis**.

You are the expert. You have spent time analyzing their brand. You are here to expose the gap, show the vision, and offer a path forward.

**No prices on this call.**  
Price is discussed in the custom proposal sent within 48 hours after the call.

**The goal:** Get them to say yes to a custom proposal.

---

## Pre-Call Research (10 Minutes)

Before every call, research:

1. **Instagram last 12 posts**
   - Content quality (phone vs. professional)
   - Posting frequency
   - Engagement rate (likes/comments vs. followers)
   - Content mix (product, lifestyle, UGC, campaign)

2. **Website/digital presence**
   - Booking/purchase flow
   - Mobile experience
   - Speed and clarity
   - Social proof (reviews, testimonials, UGC)

3. **Competitor scan (2-3 competitors)**
   - What are they posting?
   - What ads are they running? (Meta Ad Library)
   - Where are they winning?
   - What's the exploitable gap?

4. **Current state assessment**
   - Do they have a content system?
   - Are they running paid ads?
   - Who handles their creative currently?
   - What's their monthly marketing spend (roughly)?

**Write 3 specific observations before the call.** These are your ammunition.

## Call Structure (20–30 Minutes)

### Opening: Establish Authority (2–3 min)

```
"Thanks for making the time, [Name].

I've spent the last 48 hours looking at your brand, your content, and your competitive landscape. I've also looked at what [Competitor A] and [Competitor B] are doing — what's working for them and where they're leaving gaps.

Before we talk about solutions, I want to show you exactly what I see. Because there's a clear gap between where you are and where you could be, and I want you to understand what that gap is costing you."
```

**Why this works:**
- You've done homework (most people don't)
- You mention competitors (shows market awareness)
- You promise to expose a gap (creates curiosity)
- You mention cost (implies quantified loss)

---

### Part 1: The Gap Exposure (5–7 min)

Present 2–3 specific observations. Be direct but kind.

**Restaurant Example:**

```
"Here's what I see:

**Observation 1:** Your last 12 posts are all food shots. Beautiful food, but no people, no experience, no story. The algorithm doesn't push food-only content anymore — it pushes emotion and human connection. You're getting likes from foodies but not bookings from diners.

**Observation 2:** You have [X]K followers but your bio has no booking link, no clear CTA, and no reason to convert today. That's not a following — it's a missed revenue stream. If 100 people view your profile daily and even 10% want to book but can't find how, at R150 per head, that's real money invisible every week.

**Observation 3:** [Competitor Name] is running [specific campaign type — weekend promos, UGC campaigns, influencer features]. Their content feels like a campaign. Yours feels like documentation. The difference is creative direction."
```

**Fashion Example:**

```
"Here's what I see:

**Observation 1:** Your drops get saved but not shared. The save-to-share ratio tells me people want the piece but don't feel proud enough to repost it. That's a creative direction problem, not a product problem.

**Observation 2:** Your competitor [Name] creates campaign energy around every drop. Countdowns, teasers, 'sold out' stories. Your drops just appear. The difference in sell-through rate is often just the creative direction around the product.

**Observation 3:** You rely on DMs for orders. Every DM-to-order is a conversion, but every DM that doesn't convert is lost revenue. A smoother creative-to-purchase flow could 2x your monthly sales without changing the product."
```

**Hospitality Example:**

```
"Here's what I see:

**Observation 1:** Your content shows the property but doesn't sell the escape. People don't book rooms — they book feelings. Your competitors are selling 'wake up here' and 'weekend reset.' You're selling square meters.

**Observation 2:** You have beautiful reviews but zero review content strategy. No guest reaction videos, no UGC campaigns, no 'before and after' stories. Social proof is your most underused asset.

**Observation 3:** Your weekend availability is inconsistent but your content doesn't create urgency. 'Book now' is weak. '6 rooms left at this rate' is strong. The creative direction around scarcity changes occupancy."
```

**Key rule:** Every observation must be:
- Specific (not "your content is weak" — "your last 12 posts are all product shots")
- Quantifiable where possible (revenue loss in rands)
- Actionable (implies a clear fix)
- Kind but direct (no blame, just diagnosis)

---

### Part 2: The Revenue Loss (2–3 min)

Quantify the gap in rands. This is the emotional anchor.

**Restaurant:**

```
"Let me put numbers to this. If you get 100 profile visits a day and even 5% want to book but can't find how, that's 5 lost bookings per day. At R150 per head, average 2 people per booking, that's R1,500 per day invisible. R10,500 per week. R42,000 per month.

And that's just from the profile. If we add weak content, no campaign system, and no creative direction, the real number is likely higher."
```

**Fashion:**

```
"If you get 50 DMs per drop and 60% ghost after asking the price, that's 30 lost orders. At an average order value of R800, that's R24,000 per drop invisible. If you drop monthly, that's nearly R300,000 per year in friction losses alone."
```

**Hospitality:**

```
"If you have 10 rooms and average 60% occupancy on weekends, that's 4 empty rooms per weekend. At R1,200 per night, that's R9,600 per weekend invisible. R38,000 per month. R456,000 per year.

Creative direction that increases weekend occupancy from 60% to 85% would add R171,000 per year. That's the value of this gap."
```

**Key rule:**
- Use their actual numbers if they share them
- If you don't know, use conservative estimates and say "even at conservative numbers..."
- The goal is not to depress them — it's to show the size of the opportunity

---

### Part 3: The Vision (3–4 min)

Paint the picture of what premium creative direction looks like for their brand.

```
"Now, here's what the other side looks like:

**What if every post was a campaign?** Not just a photo of your product, but a piece of a larger story that builds demand over time.

**What if your content created bookings before people even knew they wanted them?** That's what happens when you have creative direction — your brand becomes the place people want to be, not just the place they find.

**What if your competitors started reacting to you instead of you reacting to them?** That's the shift from documentation to direction."
```

**Show a relevant spec ad concept:**

```
"I created this spec ad direction for a [niche] in your space. Not to sell you a template — to show you the gap between where you are and what premium creative direction looks like.

[Share screen or send image]

This is a [campaign name] concept. The goal is [goal]. The hook is [hook]. The visual direction is [description].

This is the level of thinking your brand could have consistently."
```

---

### Part 4: The Question (2 min)

```
"So here's my question:

Are you open to me putting together a custom creative direction proposal based on what we've discussed today?

It would include:
- A full brand creative audit
- 3 spec ad concepts tailored specifically to your brand
- A content gap analysis
- A 30-day content system map
- A revenue projection based on the changes we discussed

I don't have fixed packages. Every brand is different. Based on your goals, your current state, and what we've discussed, I'll put together something that makes sense for where you are right now.

Are you comfortable with that approach?"
```

**If they say yes:**
- "Great. I'll have this to you within 48 hours. What's the best email?"
- "Do you have any specific campaigns or launches coming up that this should align with?"
- "What's your rough monthly marketing budget so I can scope this appropriately?"

**If they say maybe/not sure:**
- "Totally fair. What's the hesitation — budget, timing, or not sure if this is the right move?"
- Listen. Address the specific concern.
- If it's budget: "Let's start with a Creative Diagnosis. It's a one-time audit that shows you the full gap. If it resonates, we talk about ongoing direction. If not, you still have the audit."

**If they say no:**
- "I respect that. Can I ask — what would need to change for creative direction to become a priority?"
- "Would it help if I sent you the spec ad concept anyway? No strings. Just so you have a reference for what this looks like."

---

### Part 5: The Close (1–2 min)

```
"Perfect. I'll send the proposal to [email] by [day + 2].

It will include everything we discussed, scoped specifically for [Brand Name].

After you review it, we can jump on another quick call to walk through it. No pressure — just clarity.

Sound good?"
```

## Handling Common Objections

### "We don't have budget for this right now"

```
"I understand. Let me ask — do you have budget for the revenue you're currently losing?

Because based on what we discussed, the gap is costing you roughly [R amount] per month. The question isn't whether you can afford creative direction. It's whether you can afford to keep operating without it.

That said, I can scope this to fit where you are. We can start with a Creative Diagnosis — a one-time strategic audit that shows you the full picture. Then you decide if ongoing direction makes sense.

Does that feel more manageable?"
```

### "We're already working with someone"

```
"That's great — it means you value creative. Let me ask: are they delivering campaign concepts, or are they just posting content?

Because most 'social media managers' post. Creative directors build demand. If you're not seeing a direct line between their work and your bookings/orders, there might be a gap.

I'm not here to replace anyone. I'm here to show you what the next level looks like. If your current person can get there, fantastic. If not, you know where to find me.

Would it still be worth seeing the spec ad concept?"
```

### "We do our own content"

```
"And some of it is solid — I saw [specific post] and it had good engagement. But here's the thing: doing your own content is sustainable at 2 posts a week. It's not sustainable at daily multi-format campaigns with strategic direction.

The brands that win aren't the ones that post the most. They're the ones that post with direction. Every piece is part of a larger campaign.

Creative direction doesn't replace your voice — it amplifies it. You still know your brand. I just help you turn that knowledge into campaigns that convert.

Worth seeing what that looks like?"
```

### "How much is this going to cost?"

```
"I don't have a menu of prices because every brand needs something different. Some need a full monthly creative department. Some need a one-time campaign strategy. Some need help with a single launch.

What I can tell you: I scope based on your goals, your current state, and the value of the outcome. Not based on hours or posts.

The proposal I'll send you will have clear scope, clear deliverables, and clear investment. If it doesn't make sense for where you are, you say no. No pressure.

Fair?"
```

## Tone and Energy

| Do This | Don't Do This |
|---------|---------------|
| Speak slowly and confidently | Rush or seem nervous |
| Pause after asking questions | Fill silence with chatter |
| Use "we" when talking about their brand | Talk at them, not with them |
| Show genuine curiosity | Sound scripted or robotic |
| Be direct about gaps | Be vague or sugarcoat |
| Quantify in rands where possible | Speak in abstract terms |
| Let the silence do the work | Over-explain or justify |
| End with a clear next step | Leave the call ambiguous |
